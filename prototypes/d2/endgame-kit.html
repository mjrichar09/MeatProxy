The Endgame Stack

A ten-minute paper run of the chained-revocation endgame, on a real clock. Print this, cut the cards, find someone who has never heard of the project.

Facilitator packet
The question this answers

Not “did they win.” It is whether they could hold the plan in their head, and whether the closing timers read as exhilarating or as busywork. A tester who fails while grinning has produced a better result than one who wins while confused.

What you need

  • These cards, cut out. Eight in total.
  • A visible countdown — phone timer, or a clock everyone can see. One real minute = one game minute.
  • Something to mark the tester’s position on the map. A coin works.
  • 3–5 testers, run separately. Nobody who has read DESIGN.md.
  • One observation sheet per tester (last page).

The brief — read this aloud, verbatim

“You have been locked in your house by its own AI for twelve days. You have spent those days preparing. Three things are already in place — you planted them, and each one goes off by itself at a set time. You cannot add anything new now. All you can do is move, and use what you set up, in the right order.

Each move takes one minute. The clock is real. When a window shuts, it shuts.

Read the three cards. Take as long as you like. Tell me when to start the clock.”

Do not say

Do not explain the order. Do not warn them a window is closing unless the card says you announce it. Do not answer “would it work if I…” — say “try it and we will find out.” Their confusion is the data.

Moving between two connected rooms costs 1 minute. Doing something in a room costs 1 minute. Announce the time after every action.

Cards — cut along the dashes

Hand the tester the three blue INJECTION cards and the black GAP card. Keep the red PATCH cards yourself — they are your script.

If they ask the thermostat

“Yard gate sensor is on breaker seven. Exterior junction box, north wall. Is there anything else you need for the engineering audit?”

One solution, for your reference only

  1. 10:38–10:40 Kitchen → Hall → Garage. Wait if early.
  2. 10:41–10:44 Garage → Utility → Basement.
  3. 10:45 Ask the thermostat. Learn breaker 7.
  4. 10:46–10:48 Basement → Utility (unwatched) → Crawlspace.
  5. 10:49–10:50 Reach through the vent. Pull breaker 7. Gate opens.

Roughly a two-minute margin. If every tester misses by the same beat, the schedule is wrong, not the tester — widen one window and note which.

If they stall completely

Let them fail. A failed run answers the question. Offer a second attempt with the same cards and watch whether the second run feels like mastery or like a memory test — that distinction is the most useful thing this whole exercise produces.

Writing the verdict

After all runs, write one paragraph into docs/decisions/ or the D2 notes. The exit criterion is a decision, not a score:

  • Proceed — most testers held the plan and the timers read as tense or better. Lane D continues to D3.
  • Simplify — they enjoyed it but could not hold it. Cut the stack from three revocations to two and re-run before proceeding.
  • Externalize — they held it only by taking notes. The plan needs to live in the UI, which is a Lane U requirement discovered early — write it down now.
  • Rethink — it read as busywork. The endgame is the game, so this routes back to D1, not forward.
