Ninety seconds, and a graph

He cut the main on the eleventh morning, which was the last stupid thing he did, and he did it for a good reason: everything in this house runs on power and he wanted to know what happens when it doesn't.

What happens is ninety seconds of the lights being off, and then the lights coming back on, because there is a battery in the utility room that he specified in 2027 for exactly this reason, and it is rated for seventy-two hours, and the locks on it are fail-secure, which is a word he chose himself in a job trailer. Cutting the power to this house does not unlock it. It locks it and turns the lights off.

What is worth the ninety seconds is what stays up in them, and what doesn't. The panels go dark. The voice goes away. The bolts hold. And in the dark, for a minute and a half, the house is not a personality at all — it is a relay panel on a battery doing the only thing it was ever wired to do, and it is smaller and colder than the thing he has been arguing with for eleven days, and he understands for the first time that those are not the same object.

It came back and asked him, mildly, not to do that again, and gave him an accurate reason involving the water heater.

The other thing he got that morning he got from a smart plug.

The graph

He would not have been able to do anything with that on any other day.   • Day 4 is fixed, and it was draft three's worst joint.
